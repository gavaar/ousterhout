# Sources, attribution, and coverage

This skill is inspired by *A Philosophy of Software Design* by John Ousterhout,
particularly the second edition (2021). It is independent and is not endorsed by
the author. Its rubric is an original synthesis grounded in the public primary
sources below, not verified exhaustive coverage of the full book.

## Primary sources

- **[B] Author's book page:**
  <https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/aposd.php>
  Describes the second edition, its increased emphasis on general-purpose modules,
  and the new Decide What Matters chapter. Links purchase options and the extract.
- **[E] Official second-edition extract:**
  <https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/aposd2ndEdExtract.pdf>
  The author identifies this as revised/new chapters and Clean Code comparisons,
  not the complete book. The initial rubric does not claim a full reading of this
  PDF: its vector-outlined text was not available to the research text reader.
- **[C] Author's Stanford CS 190 discussion outline (Winter 2024):**
  <https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cs190-winter24/lectures/aposd/>
  Establishes the broad principle set and explicitly cautions against taking
  defining errors out of existence too far. It is an outline, not detailed chapters.
- **[D] Author-hosted discussion with Robert Martin (2024–2025):**
  <https://github.com/johnousterhout/aposd-vs-clean-code/blob/main/README.md>
  Detailed arguments on complexity, deep and shallow interfaces, entanglement,
  comments, method length, and TDD. Distinguish Ousterhout's statements from
  Martin's opposing views; this is later discussion, not the book text.

Links and coverage were checked on November 7, 2026. Public availability is not
permission to redistribute the book or its extract. This package includes original
summaries and links, not copied chapters or bundled source documents. The MIT
license covers this package's original material, not the linked works.

## Attribution boundaries

Approval gates, ranking, Git snapshot semantics, cost thresholds, folder mode,
verification steps, and installation are this skill's workflow choices. They are
not attributed to the book. Repository-specific testing/TDD rules take precedence
over positions expressed in the author's later TDD discussion.

Avoid fabricated quotations, page references, comprehensive-coverage claims, and
mechanical complexity scores. If a user supplies an authorized book copy or notes,
use them to refine the review with edition-specific attribution; keep their text
out of the published package unless redistribution is explicitly permitted.
